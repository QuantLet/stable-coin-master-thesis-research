### 5.4 External Drivers versus Internal Spillovers

The preceding sections show which variables enter the lower-tail equations, but selection frequency does not measure how much each information block improves the model. Four specifications are therefore re-estimated for each stablecoin and date: an intercept-only benchmark, a macro-only model, a coin-only model and a joint model. The estimations retain the 90-day window, the 5% conditional quantile, window-specific median/MAD scaling and strict minimum finite GACV selection used in Chapter 4. Stablecoin deviations are measured against their own reference assets. The main specification combines contemporaneous deviations of the other ten stablecoins with the five macro-financial variables lagged by one calendar day.

The conditional results show a clear ordering. Relative to the intercept-only benchmark, the macro-only model reduces mean selected GACV by 8.3%, compared with 33.7% for the coin-only model and 37.0% for the joint model. Adding the stablecoin block to the macro-only model lowers GACV by a further 31.3%, whereas adding the macro block to the coin-only model lowers it by 5.0%; both differences remain significant after Holm adjustment ($p<0.001$). A two-block Shapley decomposition assigns 84.3% of the joint improvement to stablecoin information and 15.7% to macro-financial information. Excluding windows containing past-filled peg observations leaves the corresponding incremental reductions at 32.4% and 4.8%.

Held-out performance reverses this ordering. When stablecoin deviations are supplied on the evaluation date, quantile skill scores are −12.5% for the macro-only model, −15.9% for the coin-only model and −19.8% for the joint model; negative values indicate higher check loss than the rolling intercept-only benchmark. When every predictor is dated no later than $t-1$, the coin-only and joint scores fall to −34.4% and −38.6%. On the common 2,251 dates, using contemporaneous rather than lagged stablecoin information reduces joint-model loss by 13.5% (HAC Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$). The estimated structure describes the current lower-tail state but does not improve the following day's quantile forecast.

Taken together with Section 5.2, the evidence points to synchronous and state-dependent tail-risk alignment rather than a persistent directional propagation mechanism. Coin-to-coin information dominates the measurement of current systemic pressure, while macro-financial variables provide a smaller additional component. The centrality measures in Section 5.5 should therefore be read as positions within the contemporaneous tail-risk network, not as rankings of one-day-ahead predictive influence.
