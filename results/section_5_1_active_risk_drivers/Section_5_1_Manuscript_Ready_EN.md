### 5.1 Composition of Active Risk Drivers

The DPI measures the degree of regularisation required to summarise lower-tail dependence, but it does not identify which variables remain in the corresponding equations. To examine this composition, the active set for target stablecoin *i* on day *t* is defined as \(A_{it}=\{k:|\hat{\beta}_{kit}|>10^{-10}\}\). The analysis uses the coefficient panel underlying the primary DPI specification: a 90-day rolling window, the 5% conditional quantile, window-specific median/MAD scaling, and the strict minimum finite GACV rule. Each target equation contains ten other stablecoin deviations and five lagged macro-financial variables. Stablecoin deviations continue to be measured relative to each coin's own reference asset.

Across 24,772 target-day equations, the mean active-set size is 9.53 of 15 predictors; the median is 10, with an interquartile range of 8–11. After accounting for the unequal numbers of candidate variables, stablecoin and macro-financial predictors have nearly identical inclusion rates of 63.5% and 63.8%, respectively. Although stablecoin variables account for 66.6% of all selected terms, this raw share largely reflects that each equation contains twice as many stablecoin candidates as macro-financial candidates. The normalized rates therefore provide no evidence that either group generally dominates the lower-tail specification.

The active set becomes smaller as the DPI increases. Its average size declines from 10.11 predictors in the very-low-pressure state to 9.00 in the severe state. Over the same comparison, the inclusion rate falls from 67.4% to 60.2% for stablecoin predictors and from 67.5% to 59.6% for macro-financial predictors. By contrast, the stablecoin share of selected terms remains between 66.4% and 66.9% across all five states. Higher systemic depegging pressure is therefore associated with stronger variable selection rather than a broader set of active drivers, while the balance between internal and external predictors changes little. This pattern is descriptive: the active set and the DPI are generated by the same penalised estimation, so their state-wise association is partly mechanical and should not be interpreted as an independent regime test.

The identity of selected variables nevertheless changes over time. CVIX is the most frequently selected macro-financial predictor (74.2%), while BUSD has the highest inclusion rate among stablecoin predictors (69.2%); the one-year Treasury yield (66.3%), IDRT (65.7%) and DXY (65.6%) also enter frequently. Figure 5.1 shows substantial month-to-month variation around these full-sample averages, indicating that the lower-tail equations do not rely on a fixed set of covariates. Selection frequency measures the persistence of conditional inclusion, not coefficient magnitude, causal influence or risk transmission; correlated predictors may also substitute for one another. Sections 5.2 and 5.3 therefore examine the signed coin-to-coin links and macro-financial channels separately.
