### 5.3 Macro-Financial Conditions and Stablecoin Tail Risk

This section evaluates whether the five lagged macro-financial variables remain recurrent correlates of stablecoin lower-tail risk after controlling for the other stablecoins and the remaining macro factors. A factor is classified as active when its absolute Quantile-Lasso coefficient exceeds $10^{-10}$. For each factor, the analysis reports its selection frequency, coefficient sign and share of absolute standardized coefficient weight. Statistical inference is based on daily cross-target averages over 2,252 dates and uses Newey-West HAC covariance with a Bartlett kernel and lag 90. The coefficient-weight measure is descriptive; it is not an estimate of incremental predictive content.

CVIX is selected most frequently, appearing in 74.2% of eligible equations (HAC 95% CI, 71.3-77.1%). The corresponding frequencies are 66.3% for the one-year Treasury yield, 65.6% for DXY, 57.1% for the S&P 500 and 55.6% for VIX. Equality across the five frequencies is rejected (HAC Wald χ²(4) = 178.96, $p=1.25 \times 10^{-37}$). Selection is also uneven across stablecoin targets. After Holm correction across the five factor-level tests, target heterogeneity remains statistically distinguishable for CVIX, DXY and VIX, but not for the Treasury yield or the S&P 500. Figure 5.3 shows that these differences are not confined to the non-dollar-referenced coins; for example, DXY selection ranges from 55.2% for USDC to 75.6% for sUSD.

The coefficient signs provide a stricter test of the risk-off interpretation used in the presentation. With the reference-adjusted signed-deviation outcome, a positive S&P 500 coefficient is stress-aligned because an equity decline lowers the conditional tail, whereas negative VIX and CVIX coefficients are stress-aligned because higher volatility lowers it. Among active coefficients, 59.7% of S&P 500 coefficients have the stress-aligned sign; the positive-minus-negative frequency is 11.0 percentage points (HAC 95% CI, 5.8-16.3; Holm-adjusted $p=1.69 \times 10^{-4}$). VIX is stress-aligned in 56.6% of active cases, with a sign balance of -7.3 percentage points (-12.9 to -1.7; Holm-adjusted $p=0.041$). CVIX signs are approximately balanced, and neither DXY nor the Treasury yield has a stable full-sample direction. The results therefore do not support the stronger claim that all five macro factors load uniformly in a risk-off direction.

Selection frequency and coefficient magnitude also differ. The five macro factors jointly account for 20.8% of the total absolute standardized slope weight. DXY has the largest individual share at 5.1%, whereas CVIX has the smallest at 3.2% despite being selected most often. Strict and screened GACV rules produce identical target-factor selection ranks, and excluding windows containing past-filled deviation cells leaves the ranking similar (Spearman ρ = 0.944). By contrast, the robust-scaled and unscaled rankings are unrelated (ρ = 0.009), while the two chronological halves show only moderate agreement (ρ = 0.470). The full-sample evidence thus supports recurrent but target- and specification-dependent macro-financial associations, not a fixed causal transmission channel or general macro dominance. Section 5.4 tests the relative role of external and coin-to-coin information directly through macro-only, coin-only and joint specifications.
