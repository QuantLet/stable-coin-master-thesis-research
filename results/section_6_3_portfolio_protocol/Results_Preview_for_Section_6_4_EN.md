### Result prepared for Section 6.4

The out-of-sample results favour conventional covariance diversification rather than direct allocation on the Quantile-Lasso penalties. Over 2,162 evaluation days, GMV records annualised volatility of 3.14%, compared with 10.93% for EW. Its 5% ES is 39.03 basis points, against 101.39 basis points for EW, and its maximum drawdown is 2.60%, against 15.63%. The paired moving-block bootstrap gives a volatility difference of -7.80 percentage points (95% interval, -11.92 to -2.41; Holm-adjusted *p* = 0.006) and an ES difference of -62.36 basis points (-94.78 to -27.70; Holm-adjusted *p* = 0.006). The maximum-drawdown difference is -13.03 percentage points (-16.03 to -2.23; unadjusted *p* = 0.001).

Neither lambda-based strategy improves the two primary endpoints after multiplicity correction. Relative to EW, the LTEC volatility difference is -0.18 percentage points (Holm-adjusted *p* = 1.000) and its ES difference is +24.35 basis points (*p* = 0.282). The QTEC volatility difference is -0.04 percentage points (*p* = 1.000), while its ES is 16.82 basis points higher; the unadjusted interval excludes zero, but the comparison does not survive the six-test Holm correction (*p* = 0.064). LTEC and QTEC also require average turnover of 49.9% and 22.5% per rebalance, respectively, compared with 3.45% for GMV. These results are consistent with the earlier finding that the DPI and its coin-level penalties describe contemporaneous lower-tail structure but do not provide a stable short-horizon forecasting signal.
