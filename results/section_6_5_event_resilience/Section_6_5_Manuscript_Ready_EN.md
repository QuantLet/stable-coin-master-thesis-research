### 6.5 Portfolio Resilience around Major Tail Events

The full-sample comparison does not show whether risk reduction survives during market stress. We therefore restrict the evaluation to the same six non-overlapping events used in Section 4.4 and retain the portfolio weights generated without event-date information. The primary analysis pools days 0–30 after each event, giving 186 daily observations. Annualised volatility and 5% Expected Shortfall (ES) are compared with equal weight (EW) using 4,999 event-stratified moving-block bootstrap replications with seven-day blocks. Holm adjustment is applied across the six strategy–endpoint comparisons. Event-specific maximum drawdowns are reported descriptively because each individual window contains only 31 observations.

The pooled stress-window results strengthen the distinction between covariance diversification and direct use of the DPI penalties. GMV volatility is 4.83%, compared with 7.71% for EW, a difference of −2.89 percentage points (95% bootstrap interval: −4.37 to −1.04; Holm-adjusted *p* = 0.007). Its ES is also lower by 31.13 basis points (−53.06 to −7.55; *p* = 0.008). In contrast, LTEC raises volatility by 4.45 percentage points and ES by 55.71 basis points, while QTEC raises them by 3.97 percentage points and 63.31 basis points, respectively. All four adverse differences remain significant after adjustment (*p* ≤ 0.007). Changing the bootstrap block length to three or fourteen days does not alter these conclusions.

Event-level drawdowns show that the pooled results are not uniform across shocks. GMV produces a smaller drawdown than EW in five of the six windows, with the largest reduction during Iran–Hormuz (−3.13 percentage points), but its drawdown is 0.99 percentage points larger during the SVB-linked USDC episode. LTEC and QTEC generally increase drawdowns around KuCoin, the May 2021 sell-off, Terra and FTX. Neither strategy therefore provides clear protection during FTX, despite the positive DPI response documented in Section 4.4. LTEC is only marginally better than EW during SVB, whereas both DPI-based strategies perform better during Iran–Hormuz, when the adjusted eleven-coin DPI does not increase. The ordering of portfolio outcomes consequently does not follow the ordering of event-level DPI responses.

Leave-one-event-out estimates preserve the direction of all pooled differences but show that their precision depends on event composition. In particular, the GMV ES advantage no longer passes the adjusted threshold when either the May 2021 or Iran–Hormuz window is removed; its volatility result also weakens when May 2021 is excluded. The evidence therefore supports a stress-period benefit from covariance diversification, but not an event-invariant hedge. More importantly, coin-level DPI penalties do not form a stable short-horizon allocation signal even during events that directly affect intermediation or reserve confidence. This interpretation remains bounded by the sampled network: UST is absent from the Terra window, and the Iran–Hormuz result is sensitive to the distinction between the mixed-reference and USD-pegged systems established in Section 4.4.
